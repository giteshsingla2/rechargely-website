How to choose the right prepaid plan

Most people pick a recharge by price alone and end up paying for data they never use — or running out halfway through the month. A better approach is to start from how you actually use your phone, then find the cheapest plan that covers it.

  • Check last month's data use. Your phone's settings show mobile data used over the past 30 days. Divide by 30 to get your daily need. Our data calculator guide explains what uses the most.
  • Add a 20% buffer. Video calls, travel and software updates spike usage. A small buffer saves you from buying a top-up pack.
  • Decide on validity. If you are sure you will keep the SIM, longer validity is almost always cheaper per day.
  • Count the extras you will really use. An OTT subscription bundled into a plan only saves money if you would otherwise pay for it.

Why price per day matters more than price

Operators sell plans with different validities — 28 days, 30 days, 84 days, 365 days — which makes the sticker price misleading. Dividing the price by the number of days gives you a fair comparison.

Example: a ₹349 plan for 28 days costs ₹12.46/day. A ₹799 plan for 84 days costs ₹9.51/day — about 24% cheaper for the same daily benefits.

Also note that a "28-day month" means you recharge 13 times a year, not 12. Over a year, that extra recharge adds up — another reason long-validity plans tend to win. Every plan card on our operator pages shows the per-day price so you don't have to do the maths.

Daily data vs total data

Plans either give you a daily data limit (for example 2 GB/day) that resets every night, or a total data bucket for the whole validity (for example 50 GB for 30 days).

  • Daily data suits steady users who browse and stream a bit every day. Unused data does not carry over.
  • Total data suits people whose use is uneven — light on weekdays, heavy at weekends or while travelling.

If you regularly hit your daily limit, compare the next plan up with buying separate data packs. Two or three data packs a month usually cost more than simply moving to a higher daily-data plan.

Are long-validity plans worth it?

For most people, yes. 84-day and annual plans offer the lowest cost per day and save you from remembering to recharge every month. The trade-off is paying more upfront and being locked in if prices drop or you switch operators.

A good middle ground: if you have used the same SIM for more than a year, take the 84-day plan. If you are certain you will keep it, the annual plan is usually the cheapest option on the market.

Prepaid vs postpaid: which is cheaper?

Prepaid is cheaper for most single users because you only pay for what you choose. Postpaid makes sense when you want a family plan, need international roaming, or prefer one monthly bill. Family postpaid plans that share data across 3–4 connections can work out cheaper than separate prepaid recharges for each member. Read the full prepaid vs postpaid comparison.

If you also pay for home broadband, look at fibre plans that bundle mobile connections. Our broadband vs mobile data guide shows when combining them saves money.

Save more with cashback and credit cards

Many credit cards and UPI apps offer cashback or reward points on mobile recharges and utility bills. Before you pay, check:

  • Whether your credit card gives extra rewards on telecom or bill payments.
  • If there is a monthly cap on cashback for recharges.
  • Whether paying via UPI or wallet gives a better offer that day.

On an annual plan, even 5% cashback is worth ₹150–₹180 — enough to cover a month's data top-ups. See how to get cashback on recharges.

Switching operator without changing your number

Mobile Number Portability (MNP) lets you move to a different operator and keep your number. Send an SMS with PORT <your 10-digit number> to 1900 to receive a Unique Porting Code (UPC), then request a SIM from the new operator with that code. Frequently asked questions

Do you charge me on this website?

No. This website only helps you compare plans and save your details. You pay and complete the recharge in the Rechargely app.

Which operator has the cheapest unlimited plan?

It changes often. Use the comparison table at the top of this page — it highlights the lowest cost per day for monthly, 84-day and annual plans.

What happens if I recharge before my current plan ends?

Most operators queue the new plan so it starts when the current one ends. Check the plan details in the app before paying.

Is 1.5 GB per day enough?

For browsing, social media and some video, yes. If you stream HD video daily or use your phone as a hotspot, 2 GB/day or more is safer.

Are "free recharge" offers real?

Almost never. Messages promising a free recharge for forwarding a link or sharing an OTP are scams. Read how to spot recharge scams.
