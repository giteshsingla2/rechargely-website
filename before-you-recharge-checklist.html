Before You Recharge: A 2-Minute Checklist to Save Money

You've picked the operator ₹— plan. Before you save your details, run through these quick checks — they can save you money on this recharge and on every one after it. First, check whether your current plan is still active: most operators queue a new recharge so it starts only when the current plan ends, which means you won't lose any days by recharging early.

Pay with a credit card that rewards recharges

Mobile recharges and bill payments are fixed monthly costs, which makes them one of the easiest places to earn credit card cashback or reward points. Some cashback credit cards give a higher rate on utility and telecom payments, while others exclude them entirely — so check your card's terms before you pay in the app. On an annual plan, even 5% back is worth more than a month of data packs. Always pay your card bill in full: credit card interest costs far more than any reward. Read our full guide to cashback on recharges.

Is your phone protected?

A recharge keeps your number running, but a cracked screen or water damage can cost more than a year of recharges. Mobile phone insurance and extended warranty plans cover repairs for accidental and liquid damage, and some policies also cover theft. Compare the premium with the repair cost of your phone, check the deductible you pay per claim, and read what's excluded before you buy. Many brands and card issuers offer screen-protection plans within the first few days of purchase.

Recharging for the whole family?

If you're paying for three or four connections at home, a family postpaid plan with shared data, or a fibre broadband plan bundled with mobile connections, can cost less than separate prepaid recharges. Add up what your household spends each month and compare it with a bundle — see prepaid vs postpaid and broadband vs mobile data for the full comparison.

Why we ask for these details

  • Name and mobile number — so the app knows which number to recharge and can confirm it's you.

Your details are stored securely and used only to prepare this recharge. We never ask for your card number, UPI PIN or OTP on this website — if anyone does, it's a scam. Learn how to spot recharge scams.

What happens after you save

  1. You'll see a confirmation page with a link to the Rechargely app.
  2. Log in to the app with the same mobile number — your operator plan will be ready.
  3. Check the final price, then pay with UPI, a debit or credit card, net banking or a wallet.
  4. The operator sends a confirmation SMS once the recharge is active.

Choosing how to pay in the app

UPI is the quickest and usually free, and payment apps often run small recharge offers. A credit card earns rewards and gives you up to 45–50 interest-free days if you pay the bill in full. Net banking and debit cards work everywhere but rarely earn rewards. For large payments like an annual plan, using your best reward card makes the biggest difference.

Common questions

Will I lose my current plan's remaining days?

Usually not. Most operators queue the new plan to start after the current one ends. Check the plan details in the app before paying.

Can I recharge someone else's number?

Yes. Enter their mobile number above. They don't need the app — you can pay for their recharge from your account.

Does the price include GST?

Prepaid plan prices shown by operators already include GST. The app shows the final amount before you pay.

What if my recharge fails?

If money is deducted but the recharge fails, it is normally refunded to the original payment method within a few working days.
