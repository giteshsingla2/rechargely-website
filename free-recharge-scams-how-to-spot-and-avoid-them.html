Free Recharge Scams: How to Spot and Avoid Them

"Free 3 months recharge!" messages are everywhere. Here's how they work and how to stay safe.

Safety guide · 5 min read

In this guide
  1. Common recharge scams
  2. Warning signs
  3. How to recharge safely
  4. If you've been scammed

Common recharge scams

"Free recharge" links

A message says an operator or a political party is giving away a free recharge — just tap the link and share it with 10 friends. The link collects your details, shows ads or installs malicious apps. No recharge ever arrives.

Fake KYC or "SIM will be blocked" calls

A caller claims your SIM will be deactivated unless you update KYC, then asks you to install a screen-sharing app or share an OTP. Operators do not block SIMs this way over a phone call.

Cashback or refund requests

Someone says a recharge "failed" and they'll refund you — then sends a UPI collect request. Approving it sends money out of your account.

Fake recharge apps and websites

Look-alike apps offer impossible discounts, take payment and never recharge.

Warning signs

  • Anything "free" that asks you to forward a message.
  • Pressure to act right now.
  • Requests for an OTP, UPI PIN, CVV or screen sharing.
  • A UPI request when you were expecting to receive money. You never need a PIN to receive money.
  • Shortened or misspelled links that don't match the operator's official website.
Remember: no genuine operator, bank or recharge app will ever ask for your OTP or UPI PIN on a call or chat.

How to recharge safely

  • Recharge only through the operator's official app or website, or a recharge app you trust.
  • Check the plan and price before you pay, and wait for the operator's confirmation SMS.
  • Never install apps a caller asks you to install.
  • Keep your phone and apps updated.

On Rechargely, you only compare plans and save your details on the website. Payment happens in our app, and we never ask for your OTP, PIN or card details on this website.

If you've been scammed

  1. Call 1930, the national cyber crime helpline, as soon as possible — quick reporting improves the chance of stopping the money.
  2. Report online at cybercrime.gov.in.
  3. Tell your bank to block your card or UPI if you shared details.
  4. Report the number or message through the government's Sanchar Saathi portal.
