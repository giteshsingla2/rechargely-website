Prepaid vs Postpaid: Which Mobile Plan Is Cheaper in India?

A side-by-side look at cost, control and convenience — and the situations where switching saves real money.

The basic difference

With prepaid, you pay first and use the benefits until the validity ends. With postpaid, you use the service and receive a bill at the end of each billing cycle. Both give you the same network and the same phone number — what changes is how you pay and what extras you get.

In India, the vast majority of mobile users are on prepaid. It's simple, there's no bill shock, and you can change plans every month. Postpaid has a smaller but loyal user base, mostly families and frequent travellers who value the extras.

When prepaid is cheaper

  • You're a single user. Entry postpaid plans usually cost more per month than a comparable prepaid plan.
  • You're happy with long validity. 84-day and annual prepaid plans have a lower cost per day than most postpaid plans.
  • Your usage is predictable. If you rarely exceed your daily data, postpaid's rollover adds little value.
  • You want to avoid surprises. Prepaid can't run up a bill.

When postpaid is cheaper

  • You have 3–4 people at home. A family plan with add-on connections often costs less than four separate prepaid recharges.
  • You travel abroad often. Postpaid roaming packs and add-ons are typically easier to manage. See our roaming guide.
  • Your data use is lumpy. Rollover lets you carry unused data into heavy months.
  • You want bundles. Postpaid plans more often include streaming subscriptions and broadband bundles.
Quick test: add up what everyone at home spends on prepaid each month. If the total is higher than a family postpaid plan with the same number of connections, postpaid will save you money.

Family plans explained

A family postpaid plan has one primary connection that pays the bill and one or more add-on connections. The data is usually a shared pool, while calls and SMS are unlimited for everyone. Things to check before signing up:

  • The cost of each extra add-on connection.
  • Whether the shared data pool is big enough for the heaviest user.
  • Whether add-ons can be on different phones and who controls the account.
  • Any lock-in period or exit charges.

How to switch between prepaid and postpaid

You can usually convert between prepaid and postpaid with the same operator through its app, website or store — your number stays the same. Switching to another operator at the same time is done through number porting; see how to port your number.

Before switching from postpaid to prepaid, clear any outstanding bill. Before switching from prepaid to postpaid, use up any remaining prepaid balance, as it may not carry over.

FAQs

Does postpaid have better network speed?

Both use the same network. Some operators prioritise postpaid users during congestion, but day-to-day speeds are usually similar.

Can I go back to prepaid later?

Yes. Conversion back to prepaid is normally allowed once your bill is cleared and any lock-in period is over.

Is a credit check needed for postpaid?

Operators may ask for address and identity proof and sometimes a security deposit. Requirements vary by operator.
