How to Get Cashback on Mobile Recharge: Credit Cards, UPI and Wallets

Recharges are a fixed monthly cost — which makes them one of the easiest places to earn rewards. Here's how to do it right.

How much can you save?

Say your household spends ₹1,200 a month on recharges and broadband — ₹14,400 a year. At a 5% reward rate, that's ₹720 back every year, for doing nothing more than choosing how to pay. Prepay an annual plan of ₹3,599 with a 5% card offer and you save about ₹180 in one go.

Credit card rewards on recharges

Credit cards fall into three groups when it comes to recharges and bills:

  • Cards with an accelerated rate on bills or telecom. Some cashback and co-branded cards give a higher percentage on utility bills, recharges or payments made through a specific app.
  • Cards with a flat rate on everything. You earn the card's normal rate — still better than nothing.
  • Cards that exclude utilities. Many cards earn zero rewards on utility, telecom or wallet-load transactions. Check your card's terms for "excluded categories".

If you're choosing a new card, compare the reward rate on bills and recharges, the monthly cap on those rewards, and the annual fee. A card with a fee only makes sense if your rewards comfortably exceed it.

Pay in full, every month. Credit card interest is far higher than any cashback. Rewards only make sense if you clear the full bill by the due date.

UPI and wallet offers

Payment apps regularly run offers on recharges — a flat discount, scratch cards, or extra cashback on the first recharge of the month. These offers change often and are usually capped at a small amount. They work best for monthly plans where a ₹20–₹50 offer is a meaningful percentage.

Some credit cards can now be linked to UPI. Check whether your card earns rewards on UPI payments before relying on this.

Common catches

  • Monthly caps. "5% cashback" might stop after ₹100 a month.
  • Minimum spend. Some offers apply only above a set amount.
  • Platform-specific. The higher rate may only apply when paying through a particular app.
  • Points vs cash. Reward points can be worth much less than their face value when redeemed. Check the redemption rate.
  • Surcharges. Some platforms add a convenience fee on credit card payments that cancels out the reward.

Checklist before you pay

  1. Pick the right plan first — cashback doesn't fix a plan that's too big. Check your data needs.
  2. Check your card's reward rate and caps for recharges.
  3. Look for a UPI or wallet offer that's live today.
  4. Make sure there's no convenience fee.
  5. For annual plans, use your best reward rate — it's the biggest single payment.

FAQs

Do recharges count as utility payments?

It depends on the card issuer and the merchant's category code. Some issuers treat telecom as utilities; others treat it as general spend.

Can I stack a card offer and a UPI offer?

Usually not on the same transaction. Pick whichever gives more.

Is cashback taxable?

Cashback on your own purchases is generally treated as a discount. For specific advice, consult a tax professional.
